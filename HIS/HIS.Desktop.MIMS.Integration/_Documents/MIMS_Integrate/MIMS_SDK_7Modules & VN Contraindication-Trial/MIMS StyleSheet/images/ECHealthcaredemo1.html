  Krisovin tab 500 mg (griseofulvin/Systemic) vs phenobarbital 30mg Oral Tablet (phenobarbital/Systemic) [Use combination with caution.]

Interaction Effect

griseofulvin has its effect reduced by phenobarbital

Severity Level

Moderate - These medications may interact resulting in the potential deterioration of the patient's condition. The patient should be monitored for the possible manifestations of the interaction. Medical intervention or a change in therapy may be required.

Documentation Level

Good - Although controlled studies may not have been performed, several case reports have been documented and other data strongly suggests this interaction exists.

Probable Mechanism

Phenobarbital may reduce the serum levels and effects of griseofulvin, possibly by impairing its absorption from the gastrointestinal tract. Two cases of griseofulvin treatment failure for tinea capitis due to coadministration with phenobarbital have been reported. In a study with 16 healthy volunteers, combined use of oral griseofulvin and phenobarbital resulted in significantly lower griseofulvin AUC compared to the control group. A similar finding is also reported in another study with six healthy volunteers. However, the latter study also reported that phenobarbital did not affect plasma levels of griseofulvin when griseofulvin was administered intravenously, suggesting that the mechanism of interaction is likely due to absorption of oral griseofulvin. Monitor patient for signs of reduced griseofulvin effects. Giving griseofulvin in three divided doses daily has also been suggested to minimise this potential interaction.

Actions to be taken

  1. Use combination with caution.
  2. Monitor patient clinically.

Documentation Level

Limited - Few reports of this interaction exist. These few reports usually consist of limited case reports where clinically sound justification of the interaction is found.

Barbiturates may reduce the serum concentrations of doxycycline by inducing its hepatic metabolism. Consider increasing doxycycline dose as necessary. Alternatively, use other tetracycline antibiotics (e.g. tetracycline, methacycline).

Moderate - These medications may interact resulting in the potential deterioration of the patient's condition. The patient should be monitored for the possible manifestations of the interaction. Medical intervention or a change in therapy may be required.

Documentation Level

Limited - Few reports of this interaction exist. These few reports usually consist of limited case reports where clinically sound justification of the interaction is found.

Barbiturates may reduce the serum levels and effects of azole antifungals by inducing their CYP3A4-mediated metabolism. Interaction Effect

phenobarbital may decrease the serum concentration of oxcarbazepine

Severity Level

Caution - The interaction may occur based on the mechanism of action of the co-administered medicines. Be alert for increased or decreased effect, depending on the combination of medicines.

Documentation Level

Limited - Few reports of this interaction exist. These few reports usually consist of limited case reports where clinically sound justification of the interaction is found.

Probable Mechanism

Coadministration of phenobarbital and oxcarbazepine has been reported to decrease the serum concentration of oxcarbazepine and its active metabolite (by 43% and 25%, respectively), possibly due to increased oxcarbazepine metabolism. Additionally, oxcarbazepine increased phenobarbital levels by 14% in one report, possibly due to inhibition of the CYP2C19-mediated metabolism of phenobarbital. The clinical significance of these interactions are unclear but bear the interactions in mind in the event of unexpected response to therapy.

Actions to be taken

  phenobarbital 30mg Oral Tablet (phenobarbital/Systemic) vs Apo-Theo LA SR tab 200 mg (theophylline/Systemic) [Use combination with extreme caution.]

Interaction Effect

phenobarbital may decrease the serum concentration of theophylline

Severity Level

Moderate - These medications may interact resulting in the potential deterioration of the patient's condition. The patient should be monitored for the possible manifestations of the interaction. Medical intervention or a change in therapy may be required.

Documentation Level

Well Established - There have been several published reports of this interaction. The pharmacological explanation of why the interaction occurs is well documented and understood.There are usually controlled studies that have established that the interaction exists.

Probable Mechanism

Barbiturates may decrease the serum levels and possibly effects of theophylline by inducing its hepatic metabolism. Clinical studies have described increased theophylline clearance, reduced theophylline levels, and increased theophylline requirements during coadministration with phenobarbital. However, some studies did not find statistically significant changes, possibly due to interindividual variability and length of phenobarbital treatment. One case report in a neurosurgical patient described an increase in theophylline clearance by 1.95-fold during pentobarbital and aminophylline coadministration, resulting in several aminophylline dose increases. Another case report described an increase in theophylline clearance upon addition of secobarbital (after a lag period of approximately two weeks), then subsequent decrease in theophylline clearance after secobarbital discontinuation. Closely monitor serum theophylline levels during concurrent treatment with barbiturates and adjust the dose accordingly.

Actions to be taken

  1. Use combination with extreme caution.
  2. Monitor drug levels.
  3. Monitor patient clinically.

  Dirine inj 20 mg/2 mL (furosemide/Systemic) vs meloxicam 15mg Oral Tablet (meloxicam/Systemic) [Use combination with caution.]

Interaction Effect

furosemide has its effect reduced by meloxicam

Severity Level

Moderate - These medications may interact resulting in the potential deterioration of the patient's condition. The patient should be monitored for the possible manifestations of the interaction. Medical intervention or a change in therapy may be required.

Documentation Level

Well Established - There have been several published reports of this interaction. The pharmacological explanation of why the interaction occurs is well documented and understood.There are usually controlled studies that have established that the interaction exists.

Probable Mechanism

Coadministration of diuretics with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) may reduce the antihypertensive and diuretic effects of diuretics. This may be due to impaired prostaglandin synthesis which may lead to salt and water retention. Studies have shown that concurrent use led to decreased natriuretic effects of loop diuretics. Additionally, NSAIDs also have nephrotoxic effects which may be exacerbated by diuretic therapy. The addition of an ACE inhibitor to this combination (NSAID + diuretic) will further increase the risk of renal impairment, especially in the elderly. Monitor blood pressure and renal function during concurrent use.

Actions to be taken

  1. Use combination with caution.
  2. Monitor renal function.
  3. Monitor blood pressure.

  Dirine inj 20 mg/2 mL (furosemide/Systemic) vs Apo-Theo LA SR tab 200 mg (theophylline/Systemic) [Use combination with caution.]

furosemide has an unpredictable effect with theophylline

Severity Level

Moderate - These medications may interact resulting in the potential deterioration of the patient's condition. The patient should be monitored for the possible manifestations of the interaction. Medical intervention or a change in therapy may be required.

Documentation Level

Limited - Few reports of this interaction exist. These few reports usually consist of limited case reports where clinically sound justification of the interaction is found.

Probable Mechanism

Furosemide may theoretically increase the renal excretion of theophylline, and result in reduced theophylline serum levels. However, furosemide is reported to increase, decrease or to have no effect on theophylline levels. Theophylline has been shown to increase the diuretic effects of furosemide in infants during extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. It is prudent to monitor for changes in theophylline effects, consider measuring theophylline levels and adjust dose accordingly. As both theophylline and furosemide can cause hypokalaemia, it is also prudent to monitor serum potassium levels.

Actions to be taken

  1. Use combination with caution.
  2. Monitor patient clinically.
  3. Monitor serum potassium.
